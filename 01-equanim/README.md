# World 1 · equanim

*The gallery is the curriculum.*

[equanim](../README.md#the-six-worlds) is the studio's calm gallery of
turning mathematics — in its own words, *a gallery, not a game*. Fifty-three
catalogues of fifty-two cards each: 2,756 turning wireframes, every single
one generated by code, with not one image shipped. Alongside the catalogues
live the **Fibonacci in Motion** simulations — looping studies of the golden
ratio's habits. You open a catalogue, tap a card, drag it to turn it, and
read how it is built. And the whole gallery keeps one quiet contract: no
streaks, no daily anything, no meters, no ads, nothing expires.

This world teaches you to build that gallery's atom with your own hands: **a
turning gallery card** — a mathematical shape, held as numbers, turned by
sin and cos, drawn fresh every frame. Three lessons, one card.

(A version note: equanim itself is built in Godot 4.7; everything in these
lessons runs happily in Godot 4.3 or newer, which is all you need.)

## What you will build

| | Lesson gives you | The card gains |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Two dials — sin and cos — and parametric curves | a curve, finding its turn |
| 2 | The superformula: a circle with a personality dial | dials you can tune, a drag-to-turn gesture |
| 3 | 3D corners, two rotation dials, a shadow-flat projection | real depth — a wireframe that tumbles |

By the end of lesson 3 you have built, from an empty editor, the exact kind
of object that fills equanim's shelves — and you will know where every line
of it comes from.

## The lessons

1. **[A circle is two dials](lesson-1-a-circle-is-two-dials.md)** — sin and
   cos as two wagging dials; a circle, an ellipse, and a turning Lissajous
   figure. *You can stop as soon as the circle turns.*
   Starter: [`starter/lissajous_card.gd`](starter/lissajous_card.gd)
2. **[One formula, every flower](lesson-2-one-formula-every-flower.md)** —
   the superformula (Gielis, 2003): one equation morphing star → square →
   petal, on an inspect-card with exposed dials. *You can stop at your first
   supershape.* Starter: [`starter/superformula.gd`](starter/superformula.gd)
3. **[Wireframes that turn](lesson-3-wireframes-that-turn.md)** — a wire
   cube: eight corners in 3D, turned by two dials, flattened onto the
   screen; Euler's V − E + F = 2 as the closing check. *You can stop when
   the cube turns on its own.*
   Starter: [`starter/wire_cube.gd`](starter/wire_cube.gd)

Every lesson follows the house skeleton in [STYLE.md](../STYLE.md): see it
first, at most three new symbols, full runnable code at every milestone, and
a marked **"you can stop here"** line — stopping there counts as finishing.

## Running a starter file

1. Install Godot 4.3+ (free, [godotengine.org](https://godotengine.org)).
2. New Project → **2D Scene** (one `Node2D` appears).
3. Right-click the node → **Attach Script** → accept the name.
4. Paste a starter file's contents over everything in the editor.
5. Press **F5** and choose **Select Current** when asked. Done — no assets,
   no plugins, nothing else to download.

## Sources for this world (combined)

- **Primary:** J. Lissajous, "Mémoire sur l'étude optique des mouvements
  vibratoires", *Annales de chimie et de physique*, 1857 — the curves of
  lesson 1, first seen as light on vibrating mirrors. Scans freely readable
  via [gallica.bnf.fr](https://gallica.bnf.fr) (search the journal title and
  year).
- **Primary:** J. Gielis, "A generic geometric transformation that unifies a
  wide range of natural and abstract shapes", *American Journal of Botany*
  90(3):333–338, 2003 — the superformula of lesson 2. Free to read on the
  journal's site: [doi.org/10.3732/ajb.90.3.333](https://doi.org/10.3732/ajb.90.3.333).
- **Primary:** L. Euler, "Elementa doctrinae solidorum", 1758 — where
  V − E + F = 2, lesson 3's closing check, was first stated.
- **Primary:** Euclid, *Elements*, Book XIII (c. 300 BC) — the five regular
  solids, lesson 3's go-deeper. Free online in D. Joyce's Clark University
  edition:
  [aleph0.clarku.edu/~djoyce/java/elements/elements.html](http://aleph0.clarku.edu/~djoyce/java/elements/elements.html).
- **Secondary (free):** Daniel Shiffman, *The Nature of Code*,
  [natureofcode.com](https://natureofcode.com) — the Oscillation chapter:
  sin, cos, polar drawing, at a kind pace, with running sketches.
- **Secondary:** J. Dennis Lawrence, *A Catalog of Special Plane Curves*,
  Dover, 1972 — the field guide where lesson 1 and 2's shapes live among
  their named relatives.
